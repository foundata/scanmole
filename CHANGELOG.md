## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- Deskew is now ScanMole's own job by default, on every device, and which mechanism does it is an explicit choice rather than a consequence of what a driver happens to expose. `--deskew-method auto|scanmole|scanner` (with a matching "Deskew method" selector in the GUI) names the owner: `auto` picks, which means ScanMole unless the scanner deskews in a way nothing can switch off, `scanmole` insists on it, and `scanner` hands the job to the device and refuses the scan if the device cannot take it. Exactly one mechanism ever straightens a page; the ones nobody chose are switched off rather than left at a device default, and ocrmypdf is asked to deskew only for a batch neither of the others owned a page of. The reason for the default is what is known rather than what is better: a backend's correction is a black box nobody has measured, while ScanMole's path behaves the same everywhere and has corpus evidence behind it. A backend mechanism can earn the automatic job by passing a documented qualification gate that measures residual angle against printed targets, with an oracle that does not use the same tool ScanMole measures with; the gate is in the scanner evidence kit, it is keyed on the driver together with the option rather than on any device name, and no mechanism has passed it yet. ScanMole measures the angle with Tesseract, which it already requires, and rotates the raw frame before anything else reads it. Under automatic page size the order is crop, rotate, crop: the second crop removes what the rotation lines up along the edges, which on a skewed 1-bit feed recovered about 10 mm of width the first crop could not resolve at all. A fixed page size keeps its configured dimensions exactly and is still straightened wherever that is safe for its content, and `--from-images` is unchanged. What this does not promise is that every page ends up straight: a page with too little text carries no angle to measure, a skew under 0.1 degrees is not worth the resampling, and a 16-bit or very large raster is declined outright. Declined pages in a batch the host otherwise straightened keep their skew and are named in a warning rather than resampled a second time during OCR. A page that cannot be measured or straightened at all (an unreadable frame, a measurement that hangs, a full disk during the rotation) ends the run as a processing failure with the scanned pages preserved for `--from-images` recovery, never as a silent success, and missing Tesseract is reported before any paper moves. Three device listings are now refused before the paper moves rather than guessed at, because one correction per page cannot be established from them: a deskew option that is read-only and already on when `--deskew-method scanmole` or `--no-deskew` was asked for (previously the pages were straightened twice, or reported as unmodified while the scanner had modified them), an option that will not say what it is doing, and two such options at once. Measured on a ScanSnap iX100, real skews of 0.13 to 1.80 degrees came back within 0.023 degrees of straight. Replaying the Brother ADS-4550W corpus, which has no backend deskew and so already took this path, moved no blank verdict and left the detected page size unchanged on 74 of its 75 frames. On the ScanSnap iX500, whose backend does offer deskew, the default moves from the scanner to ScanMole: over its 98-frame corpus one page initially changed from kept to blank, the print pack's deliberately borderline P1 sparse sheet, whose single printed line sits at the blank-detection cutoff by design. Nothing was cropped away; its geometry was corrected from a wrong 110.7 mm strip to a correct A4 page, and the same ink over a larger area reads as fainter. The sparse-content rescue below now keeps that page at the defaults; `--keep-blanks`, a higher `--blank-threshold` and a fixed `--page-size` remain the knobs for pages the rescue cannot vouch for. Pillow becomes the engine's only Python runtime dependency; 16-bit color is left alone rather than silently reduced to 8-bit, and keeps the previous behavior.
- Produced PDFs now name their origin: `Creator` becomes `ScanMole <version> by foundata`, and on OCR runs `OCRmyPDF <version> / ScanMole <version> by foundata / Tesseract ...`, since ocrmypdf composes that field itself and always puts itself first. `Producer` keeps naming the tool that wrote the bytes.
- The GUI's main window keeps what a scan usually needs; page size, deskew, archival PDF/A, the feeder switch, the hardware button mapping and the preferred paper family moved into the settings dialog, reachable from an Advanced group as well as the menu. "Combine scans" and "Auto-start when paper is inserted" moved out of their own group and sit directly above the Scan button, since both answer what pressing it does, and the wide layout now splits after the Scan card: one whole scan on the left, and what happens to the result afterwards (Output, Processing, Advanced) on the right. A first start opens wide and tall enough for that split, so every setting is visible without resizing; a remembered window size still wins. Every setting keeps its value and its meaning.
- The GUI's filename preview reads the selected output folder and shows the first name that is actually free, instead of always rendering the template with counter 1. It stays advisory: the CLI reserves the real name at scan start by creating it exclusively, so a name taken in between simply moves the run to the next candidate. The preview refreshes on template, folder, scanner, visibility, scan-exit and directory changes rather than on a timer, stops watching while the window is hidden, and never creates a file or a folder. An output name that is a symlink now counts as taken by both the preview and the reservation, so a link inside the chosen folder can no longer redirect the finished PDF somewhere else.
- The GUI's log Copy button sits beside the Log expander it belongs to rather than at the opposite edge of the row, and renders at the same weight.
- The GUI's About dialog points at the source code, the issue tracker and the project's funding page beside the website, aligned as a two-column listing wide enough to render every link whole. Its description says what ScanMole does for the reader (a document scanner for Linux with an automation-grade CLI and an optional GUI, producing small searchable PDFs, detecting blank pages, cropping and straightening) rather than which subsystems it drives. Each version row now says which interface it belongs to, and the licence name links to the project's licensing terms. The engine version appears as soon as `scanmole --version` answers, instead of waiting for the device search it used to travel with: on a network backend `scanimage -L` can run for the full two-minute listing timeout, and the dialog named no engine at all until it finished.
- Several GUI labels say more plainly what they mean: the filename preview is subtitled "Expected filename", the feeder switch reads "Turn off to scan single sheets", and ScanMole describes itself as an "Easy-to-use document scanner". The main window's credit line names the interface it reports the version of ("ScanMole (GUI) 1.1.0 by foundata"). The German translation no longer addresses the reader directly, so "Nächstes Blatt einlegen" rather than "Legen Sie das nächste Blatt ein"; the convention is written down for translators in [`DEVELOPMENT.md`](DEVELOPMENT.md#translations).

### Fixed

- A sparse page with genuine printed content is no longer dropped as blank when its few words dilute the whole-page mean above the threshold. A page the mean rule would drop now gets one guarded second look: an in-memory ink mask is searched for locally coherent, print-shaped regions, and only evidence that is localized on both axes, at least about 2.5 mm in each direction, stroke-like rather than a filled blob and itself darker than the configured threshold can overturn the verdict. Measured on the raw corpora, that keeps the deliberately borderline sparse P1 sheet on both the ScanSnap iX500 and the Brother ADS-4550W in every run that used to drop it, while boundary bars, trailing-edge shadows (whole or broken into fragments), punch holes on blank backs, sensor noise and backing texture all still classify as blank. The page raster, its geometry and every event field stay exactly as they were; the `page` event's `mean` carries the evidence mean on a rescued page, so the event explains the verdict. What this deliberately does not promise: a page whose only content is a full-width rule, a solid stamp, a lone mark under 2.5 mm (a small page number included) or strokes too faint for the fixed ink cutoff stays classified by the mean alone, because nothing short of an artifact classifier separates those from scanner artifacts; `--keep-blanks`, a higher `--blank-threshold` and `--blank-threshold 0` remain the remedies there, and `--from-images` input is untouched.
- Host deskew no longer clips content that its safety evidence can establish. Pillow turns a page inside its own canvas, so content near a corner used to leave the canvas and silently vanish, about a quarter of a 20 by 20 px corner mark already at half a degree. The turn now checks its coherent content evidence first: a turn that keeps every meaningful region aboard runs exactly as before, byte for byte; one that would clip shifts the rotated page by a few pixels inside the same canvas instead; and a page that cannot be kept whole either way, such as content in all four corners, declines the turn with a warning and keeps its skew, finally, so ocrmypdf is never handed the same unsafe rotation. Pages are never scaled and fixed page sizes keep their exact configured dimensions. Edge-hugging boundary bars and trailing shadows are the scanner's and never hold a turn hostage; over the three device corpora six pages gained a preventive shift of at most 22 px: their content ink stayed within resampling noise, the outermost band along the shift direction exchanges rotation fill for the paper's own texture (the largest measured band-mean change is 11 of 255 levels over the outermost 0.2 mm, with the side bands byte-identical and no visible border at 1:1 or page scale), and every other straightened page came out byte-identical. What this deliberately does not promise: strokes lighter than half brightness are invisible to the evidence mask, and a declined page keeps its skew rather than being straightened at content's expense.
- The GUI's "Reset to defaults" restores the window size every time, not only the first time in a session. GTK resizes a window only when the default-size property it is given actually changes, and that property does not follow a resize the user performed themselves, so after one reset it already held the default and every later reset silently left the window at whatever size it had been dragged to. Nothing else about the reset changed.
- `--keep-images` failures now end the run as the processing failure they are (exit code 5, matching `error` event) instead of a generic crash: creating the archive destination and copying each kept page are covered by the same error translation as reserving the batch directory. An all-blank run no longer leaves an empty archive directory behind, and a batch whose copy failed is removed again best-effort, since the original pages survive in the preserved work directory under the ordinary recovery contract.
- The GUI recovers its device coordination immediately when the scan process cannot be started (for example when the `scanmole` CLI was uninstalled in between). The scan takeover had already stopped the idle sensor poller and reset the advisory capability state for a scan that then never ran, and nothing restarted the capability negotiation: hardware-button and insert-to-scan polling could silently resume against the scanner's default source instead of the selected one. The failure now starts a fresh capability probe for the selected device right away, and sensor polling stays off until that probe's source-matched evidence is in. The error message itself is unchanged.
- Automatic page size no longer keeps scanner backing that happens to look like paper for a single scan line. The side edges now need 2 mm of sustained paper brightness before the walk stops, so a saturated sensor strip at the frame edge or backing noise touching the cutoff can no longer end it: measured over nine feeds at 150, 300 and 600 dpi this recovers several millimetres of width on ScanSnap iX100 gray frames and about 70 mm on a receipt scanned in a full-width window, and changes nothing on the Brother ADS-4550W or ScanSnap iX500. The walk reads brightness, so it applies to Gray and Color acquisition, including the software-lineart path where the device delivers gray and ScanMole converts to 1-bit. Frames a scanner delivers already in 1-bit carry no brightness edge to find and take the ink-density path added below. As a documented consequence, dense content closer than 2 mm to the paper edge can be cropped with the backing; select a fixed page size to bypass edge detection entirely.

### Added

- ScanMole now says when the optional `jbig2enc` encoder is missing, so black and white PDFs come out as small as they could. ocrmypdf uses it during its optimization pass to recode 1-bit pages losslessly, and finds it on `PATH` by itself, so the only thing to do is install it. The note appears exactly where it applies and nowhere else: an OCR run over black and white pages on a system without the binary. The CLI logs one line before the OCR step, and the GUI shows a hint under the OCR switch and repeats it beside the finished file. It stays advisory: nothing is required, refused or changed about the scan.
- Automatic page size now cuts native black and white scans too. A scanner with its own 1-bit mode thresholds the frame before ScanMole sees it, so there is no brightness left to walk and the dark scanner backing used to be kept as part of the page; the paper boundary is now found in the ink instead. A side is removed only when a boundary dark over most of the perpendicular axis sits within 12 mm of the frame edge and has 2 mm of sustained paper behind it. The bands have to agree on one boundary running the length of the edge, rather than each finding darkness somewhere, so a page skewed by a fraction of a degree still resolves while an isolated mark, a punch hole or a short rule cannot decide the crop, however deep it sits. Measured on a ScanSnap iX100, an A4 feed comes out 209.2 x 295.3 mm instead of the 219.5 x 295.9 mm scan window with every printed target intact; on a ScanSnap iX500 it removes the trailing-edge shadow band. Detected boundaries are kept to the pixel on every side: 1-bit rows are repacked rather than sliced from a byte boundary, so a left edge that does not fall on a byte no longer costs up to seven columns of paper (0.59 mm at 300 dpi). The same repacking makes `--page-size` content crops honor their box exactly, where a 1-bit left edge used to round outward. Gray and Color output is unchanged byte for byte. As on the brightness path the evidence is a paper edge, not content recognition: content genuinely spanning most of an axis, an intentional dense border printed along the paper edge or a barcode running nearly the full height of the page, is indistinguishable from scanner backing and is cropped with it, as is anything touching such a border. A localized mark with paper around it is not, so select a fixed page size where preserving edge-spanning content matters more.
- `--sheet-flow single|stack|collect`: how many physical sheets one run acquires. `stack` keeps today's behavior (drain the loaded feeder once), `single` scans exactly one sheet (both sides on a duplex source; the rest of a loaded stack stays in the tray), and `collect` keeps one run, and one PDF, open across scanner reloads: a feeder with a paper sensor continues automatically when the next sheet is inserted, flatbeds and sensorless devices continue on `next` via standard input or a fresh press of the scanner's own button, and `done` (or end of input) finishes the collection. The scanner is never started against an empty sensed feeder, waits are bounded by a 15 minute idle timeout, and a new additive `waiting` JSON event reports each actual wait with segment-aware sheet counts.
- The GUI's Scan action is now a split button: the primary click uses the persisted choice from two switches ("Scan all pages in feeder", on by default and gated to feeder sources, scans one physical sheet when off, and "Combine scans" keeps adding to the same PDF until Finish), and its menu starts one scan with an explicit flow ("Scan one sheet", "Scan loaded stack", "Combine scans") without changing any setting. A waiting collection shows how many sheets are in, with Finish and, where continuing is manual, Next Sheet actions.
- The GUI's automatic scanner search no longer stops once a device is found: a slower passive presence check (every 45 seconds; no device is opened, so standby is never disturbed) notices the selected scanner disappearing into standby or being unplugged, reports it and disables Start, picks it up again on wake, and also notices additional scanners plugged in later. Unchanged results are applied without any visible churn.
- The GUI exposes archival PDF/A output as its own Processing switch (previously every GUI scan silently took the CLI default), and the paper source is split into the two choices it really is: a source (Flatbed or Automatic Document Feeder) and the sides to scan (Front, Back, Both), so a device's real capabilities are visible per axis instead of spread over four combined options.
- Hardware scan buttons and insert-to-scan in the GUI: on devices exposing the sensors, an idle poller can start a scan from a press of the scanner's own button (a settings-dialog preference mapping it to the form's flow, a single sheet or collecting) or when a sheet is inserted; presses made while a scan runs or Start is blocked are ignored, and polling never interferes with discovery or capability probes. The button follows the Scan button by default, so a profile that never chose a mapping gets it; an explicitly saved "off" is kept, and insert-to-scan stays off. Polling starts only where the device exposes the sensor an enabled preference reads, so the default costs nothing on a scanner without a scan button.


## [1.1.0] - 2026-08-20

### Added

- Content-based automatic page size: where neither the device nor software edge detection can find the paper boundary (white ADF backings as on the Epson DS series, white flatbed lids), pages are now sized from their printed content, snapping to standard paper sizes with a batch majority vote. Detection is judged per axis, so a device that shortens only the paper length still gets its width sized, and an observed hardware extent pins the standard size against both sparse content and the batch majority.
- `--lineart-threshold auto` (GUI: the new "B/W (faint)" mode): one guarded global threshold per page recovers wholly faint originals such as thermal-paper receipts and washed-out copies (a page mixing normal print with a much fainter region keeps a single cut and can still lose the faint part; Gray mode remains the reliable choice there), while blank detection and page sizing keep behaving like the fixed default, with the guarded blank rescue below as the one exception.
- The GUI keeps searching for scanners automatically (every 15 seconds) until one is found, so plugging in the device after starting the application just works.
- Hardware paper detection on the `epsonds` backend: `auto` page size requests the device's ADF auto cropping (`--adf-crp`), and `--deskew` drives its hardware skew correction (`--adf-skew`).
- "B/W (faint)" now always preserves faint shades during acquisition: it prefers a recognized native text enhancement (Epson Text Enhanced Technology, Fujitsu SDTC as on the ScanSnap iX500/iX100), otherwise scans Gray or Color at 8 bit for the guarded adaptive conversion, and refuses devices that can only deliver plain 1-bit instead of quietly losing the faint shades. The final result stays 1-bit B/W on every path. A page whose entire content is too faint for the fixed threshold is no longer unconditionally dropped as blank: it survives when the adaptive result shows locally coherent text-like content, while noise-only pages still drop.
- `--auto-size-preference iso|north-american` (GUI: a preference dropdown next to the page size): decides whether ambiguous automatic page sizes resolve to the ISO A series or to Letter/Legal when the content fits both. A tie-break only, defaulting to ISO; detected paper bounds always win.
- Capability negotiation: the engine, CLI and GUI now share one support model (native, emulated in software, degraded, unsupported, unknown). The CLI warns once per scan when a fallback loses something (naming the consequence, e.g. "backs will not be scanned") and only notes equivalent software emulation; the GUI grays out degraded and unsupported source/mode choices after probing the selected device and disables Start with a reason when a saved choice is unavailable, instead of silently changing it.


### Changed

- Deskew is on by default and works on every device through a cascade: the device's own deskew where the backend offers it, otherwise straightening during OCR, otherwise a warning; the request is never a silent no-op anymore. `--no-deskew` turns it off, and the GUI got a matching toggle.
- `--keep-images` archives each batch into its own subdirectory named after the output file (`scan/`, `scan_2/`, ...), so reused and concurrent archive directories no longer overwrite or mix batches.
- `--from-images` now honors `-r/--resolution` as the one uniform input dpi for the whole batch (previously the pages were rebuilt at img2pdf's 96 dpi assumption and changed size). This deliberately overrides any resolution metadata embedded in PNG/JPEG inputs; the recovery command printed after a failed run names the correct `-r` value for rebuilding.
- GUI/CLI compatibility is now directional: an older GUI still drives any newer same-major CLI, but a newer GUI refuses an older CLI (whose options and behavior it would exceed) with a clear version message instead of failing mid-scan.


### Fixed

- Flatbed-only devices (e.g. Canon CanoScan LiDE 220) no longer batch-scan endlessly when a feeder source is requested: single-choice source options are parsed correctly and missing sources degrade with a warning.
- Nearly empty pages on full scan windows are no longer dropped as blank: blank detection measures inside the detected content area, where one is found, instead of the whole frame.
- Interrupting a run (Ctrl-C, SIGTERM) or an unexpected error no longer deletes already-scanned pages; they are preserved for recovery like other failures. Pages the scanner had already announced also finish their processing before the interrupt takes effect, so recovery can no longer race a page that is still being analyzed.
- A slow backlog of page processing can no longer race the end of the batch; every scanned page reaches the PDF even when the scanner finishes first.
- Image transformations and GUI settings are written atomically; a full disk or an interrupt can no longer corrupt the only copy of a scanned page or reset the GUI preferences.
- A failing device discovery (e.g. access denied) is reported as an error instead of being presented as "no scanners found" with a success exit code.
- Nonsensical numeric arguments (a dpi of 0, negative despeckle radius, NaN blank threshold, 0x0 page sizes) are rejected as usage errors instead of crashing later or silently changing behavior.
- PDF page sizes are now always derived from the resolution the scanner actually used: stepped resolution ranges snap to their advertised grid, a backend fixed to one dpi is honored (and reported) even when its option cannot be set, and a device without any usable resolution information is refused before feeding paper instead of producing wrongly sized pages.
- A scanner offering only a single paper source (e.g. the ScanSnap iX100's front-side feeder) no longer leaves the GUI's Start button disabled behind an unavailable saved choice: the sole source is selected automatically, and the saved preference still applies on scanners that support it.
- Cancelling or closing the GUI mid-scan waits for the engine's cleanup instead of killing it halfway or leaving it running invisibly; malformed CLI events can no longer freeze the device search.
- The GUI can no longer miss the end of a scan's event stream: the result summary and error details are always delivered before the exit is reported, a pipe kept open by a stray helper process cannot stall completion, and an invalid byte in the CLI's output no longer silently stops the log and progress updates.
- Release artifacts are built litter-free: the 1.0.0 GUI wheel had shipped stale mypy cache files (harmless, but 262 KB of dead weight).
- The OCR install hints now include the tesseract OSD data package (Fedora: `tesseract-osd`, Debian/Ubuntu: `tesseract-ocr-osd`).
- White page margins that the scanner clips to full brightness are no longer stripped as end-of-paper padding, which could delete content sitting near the paper's lower edge and shrink A4 pages toward Letter; such heights are now resolved by content-based automatic sizing instead.
- A page-processing error during a scan whose `scanimage` ignores termination is reported immediately instead of after the one-hour scan timeout (and no longer misreported as a timeout).
- Interrupting a scan just after a page-processing failure no longer reports the interrupt instead of the failure; the first cause keeps the diagnosis.
- A page the scanner finished writing but never announced (the interrupt beat its batch print) is no longer deleted with the work directory; it survives a failure and the recovery command covers it, with a note that it went through no blank detection or sizing. An incomplete raster (scanimage's `.part` staging file) is never preserved on its own.
- Quitting the GUI (e.g. Ctrl+C) with a stuck scan running now stops the scan's process group synchronously before the application exits; previously the kill escalation relied on timers that stop with the main loop, so such a scan could survive the GUI.
- Switching scanners while another device's capability probe was still running can no longer show the new scanner with the old one's source availability; every device's own probe now runs first.
- Simplex scans on devices with huge advertised windows (the Brother ADS-4550W reports a 3-metre one) no longer come out as Legal-sized pages with black side bars: on recognized feeder sources the paper edges are now found from the leading edge when the padded window drowns the ordinary detection.
- Automatic page sizing no longer shaves a fraction of a millimetre off paper edges it never detected, so content touching an undetected frame edge keeps its outermost rows; the shave is also computed from the resolution the scanner actually used, so a snapped resolution can no longer widen it several-fold.
- A narrow receipt strip whose length the scanner measured (hardware lower-edge detection) is no longer widened to a standard paper width; the measured length is kept, the width follows the printed content, and with `--keep-blanks` the strip's blank back now gets the same size as its front instead of a full-window page.
- The GUI's Scan button is disabled while the device search is still running or found no scanner, instead of launching a scan that can only fail.
- Closing, restarting or interrupting the GUI while a device search or capability probe hangs no longer leaves that helper process running, which could keep the scanner busy indefinitely; starting a scan now stops any still-running advisory probe before the engine takes over the device, instead of racing it.
- The desktop entry the GUI installs now escapes the pinned executable path exactly as the freedesktop.org specification requires (string layer and quoting layer, literal percent signs doubled), so paths containing backslashes or percent signs no longer produce an invalid or misinterpreted `Exec` line.
